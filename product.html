Robotics, without the pipeline

What game engines did for games, we do for robotics: drivers, calibration, simulation, timing and sim-to-real disappear, and what's left is the fun part. Making a robot come alive. These are mockups of how that should feel. Nothing on this page is built yet except the engine underneath.

Builda kit of parts

Start something new

Build is meant to inspire new robots, not tweaks to old ones. You start from a kit: a small set of characterized parts that snap together, so anything you make simulates accurately and can be built for real. Pick a kind of robot to start from, then assemble it by describing it, by snapping parts together, or both. The two ways edit the same robot file.

The LLM can only use the kit. It works through three tools, "add part", "connect" and "check", so it can't invent a part, and nothing runs until the checks pass.

Each archetype brings its motion once. A personality head uses MotionCore, which exists. Balancing needs a balance primitive, legged robots a policy, mobile robots a drive primitive. Each is built once, and then every robot of that kind has it.

Characterize once, inherit everywhere. A part is a data file: its BAM model, latency, limits and how to find its zero. Adding one is a bench procedure, not code.

Capabilities come from the assembly. An IMU and two wheels make balance; a camera on a chain of joints makes gaze.
