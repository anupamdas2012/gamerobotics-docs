One tick, every robot

gamerobotd is the only process that touches a gameRobotics robot. Every 20 ms it reads every joint, decides, and writes. Anything that closes a control loop runs inside that tick, so what a policy did in simulation is what it does on the robot.

System map

Five processes, one owner

Perception and behaviour live in apps. gamerobotd owns the robot: LexJR, the pan tilt, the two-wheeled rover or the rover with the pan tilt on top, each a robot file; on the rover, its diff drive turns the motion into wheel speeds. The box on the right is whatever RobotIo is plugged in: the MuJoCo body today, Feetech servos next. Nothing to its left changes when it does.

The tick20 ms

Read, decide, write, every period

read
every joint (and IMU) in one transaction
apply
client messages since the last tick, in order, never blocking
decide
joints mode, or MotionCore on this tick's positions; on a rover, odometry in and wheel speeds out
safety
finite, within limits, within speed (a wheel: within speed and acceleration); the only writer
write
goal positions; state to subscribers, dropped if they lag

A failed read writes nothing that tick. Torque comes on only when someone enables the robot, and then it holds the pose it is in.

On the Intel MacBook with the whole demo running, the CPU throttles to 20% within minutes; ticks then reach 10–50 ms and up to a fifth are skipped. The simulator, its viewer and MediaPipe are the load; the daemon's own work is about 30 µs.

Motiongr-motion

The eyes lead, the body follows

robot.look_at hands the joints to MotionCore, which runs inside the tick:

body
whole-chain aim, 8° deadband, springs 0.9 / 1.4 Hz
layers
idle sway, and the approach in metres (0.8 Hz): LexJR leans (≤ 30°, about 5 cm), a rover drives along its line of sight
tilt
the head's roll about its gaze, as the solver's target (2 Hz, ≤ 20°)
head
aimed against the commanded body, 5 Hz spring
saccade
or a jump: a quintic from the present velocity, 60 ms + 2.5 ms/°
trim
integrates measured gaze error once still (gravity sag)
solver
damped least squares: gaze, then the tilt (level by default), then posture

Kinematics come from a native chain exported from the MJCF; --kinematics mujoco uses MuJoCo itself instead. A rover's chain is its base: base_x, base_y (slides) and base_yaw, aimed from where odometry says it is.

App architecture

How an app is built

The daemon moves; apps decide. An app turns what the robot sees into intents. The gamerobotics Python SDK supplies the robot-neutral parts (Percept, Behaviour, Intent, Runner, the client), and LexJR's app supplies its perception and its behaviours. Details and code are in app-architecture.md.

slots
gaze, approach, tilt: one per motion primitive
owners
one behaviour per slot; the first in the list that wants it
share
different slots run together: one looks, one keeps the distance
replace
the same slot: select or cycle switches live
drop
intents for slots a behaviour does not own are ignored
handover
the new owner starts from the last target; 0.5 s glide, no saccades

Face follow: three behaviours, three slots

SmoothFollow says "look there" every tick, and the daemon's head spring turns it into a glide.

Example 2

Cat gaze: when to look, not how

Darts are saccades to an eye or the mouth with ±1.5° of scatter. When to jump is the behaviour's choice; the jump itself is the daemon's saccade. The attention cone is anchored on the face, so a dart to the mouth is not mistaken for you moving.

Switching between them: m calls Runner.cycle("gaze"). Cat gaze starts by staring where the robot already looks, so nothing moves until it decides to; going back, smooth follow glides to your face. Approach and tilt never stop.

Behaviourscompared

Two gaze behaviours, one face

A motion primitive says how the body moves and lives in the tick: look_at, glide or saccade, approach, tilt. They say what to do, never how a body does it, so the same behaviours run on LexJR, the pan tilt and the rover. A behaviour decides what to do and when, from what the robot sees, and is a plugin. The Runner gives each slot to one behaviour and switches them live.

SlotBehaviourSends
gazeSmoothFollow or CatGaze, switched with mrobot.look_at
approachKeepDistancerobot.approach
tiltMimicTiltrobot.tilt

Switching gaze glides between the two behaviours' targets for half a second, with saccades held back, so the robot never snaps. Whatever a behaviour sends, the daemon's Safety still clamps.

Tried live with the webcam: the cat found the face in a quarter second and cycled through track, dart and stare for nearly seven minutes, with the lean (now the approach) and tilt alongside. Quit other camera apps first; with OBS open, the webcam gave almost no frames.

Use cases

What you do with it

Demo

Have LexJR follow your face

# lexjr-sim, four terminals
.venv-bam/bin/mjpython body_server.py
../gamerobotics/target/release/gamerobotd --io sim
.venv-cam/bin/python face_cam.py
.venv-bam/bin/python face_follow.py

It looks at you, leans to keep its distance, and mirrors the tilt of your head (gamerobotics play lexjr does all four; play rover drives the rover instead). Press ] in the viewer for the side view, and c in the camera preview to set your normal distance.

Bench

Move one joint by hand

gamerobotctl enable
gamerobotctl joints neck_pitch=20 head_yaw=-15
gamerobotctl watch --hz 5
gamerobotctl disable

Degrees on the command line, radians on the wire. Safety clamps to the robot file's limits and speeds.

App

Write a behaviour

class Peekaboo(Behaviour):
    name = "peekaboo"
    slots = frozenset({"gaze"})

    def tick(self, seen):
        if seen.face is None:
            return [Intent.look_at(None)]
        return [Intent.look_at(seen.face.eyes, saccade=True)]

Subclass Behaviour from the gamerobotics package and add it to a Runner. Or skip the SDK: any language that writes a JSON-RPC line to the socket can drive the robot.

New robot

Describe it, don't code it

[[joint]]
name = "torso_yaw"
limit_deg = [-57, 57]
max_speed_dps = 183.33333333333334

[motion]
chain = "lexjr.chain.json"
config = "lexjr.motion.json"

A robot file and a RobotIo backend. The daemon, API and apps stay as they are. A wheeled robot adds mode = "velocity" joints and a [base].

Invariantstested

Rules the code keeps

  1. Nothing moves because a process started; enabling holds the present pose.
  2. The loop never waits on a client.
  3. A tick whose read failed writes nothing.
  4. Safety is the only writer, and owns the only RobotIo.
  5. Late ticks are skipped and counted, never caught up.
  6. Intents while disabled are dropped, never replayed.
  7. The robot file's limits are never tighter than the motion's.
  8. The Rust motion matches its Python reference to 1e-9 rad.
  9. Wheels stand still unless asked: enabling and leaving gaze mode stop them; a non-finite wheel target is 0.
  10. A quiet controller stops the wheels: the body stops one 0.1 s after its last target.
Decision log

How it got this shape

2026-10-16

Our own daemon, not a fork of microduck or Reachy Mini: one daemon for every robot.

2026-10-16

JSON-RPC over a unix socket, not Zenoh. No broker; permissions are access control; nothing latency-critical crosses it.

2026-10-16

No microduck dependency. Its Apache-2.0 code may be copied and credited; its policies should load later.

2026-10-16

Reachy Mini later, by bridge, as a way in for its users, without Zenoh in the loop.

2026-10-18

Gaze in Rust, in the tick. Our own solver (the k crate is single-task and URDF-only), with MuJoCo as a selectable fallback.

2026-10-18

Golden tests from MuJoCo and the Python: a port is right only if it is the same numbers.

2026-10-19

Simulator timeout 2 s, not 200 ms: a slow simulator is still alive.

2026-10-19

Saccades as a motion primitive, from the head's present velocity; when to jump is a behaviour's choice.

2026-10-19

Behaviours as plugins: a Python SDK and a Runner with one owner per slot and live, gliding switches. Cat gaze is the second gaze behaviour.

2026-10-20

A second robot, files only. The Pan Tilt Robot Test (two of LexJR's servos, a new base plate and camera yoke) runs on the same daemon, solver and behaviours. No daemon or solver code changed; lexjr-sim's robot file gained [robot], and projects gained [env].

2026-10-21

A third robot, a rover, and a body-neutral API. Two servos in wheel mode and a camera, on the same daemon and behaviours. The user chose robot.approach in metres (replacing robot.lean), the base's pose in the gaze chain with a diff drive in the daemon, and unlimited travel. Joints gained modes; the body protocol is v2.

2026-10-22

One face, four robots. The user's face, captured once from the webcam, plays back on every robot without the camera; a separate recorder films the runs after the fact, framed on the bounding box of the interaction. The showreel (media) plays one capture on all four robots, narrated with ElevenLabs.

2026-10-22

Two robots in one, no daemon change. The rover with the pan tilt on top (a head that glances, a base that turns after it and drives to keep its distance) runs from robot, CAD and test files alone. A target straight behind can hunt between two mirror-image poses; fixing that would be a MotionCore change.

2026-10-19

Mimic head tilt, a third behaviour on the same camera: the solver's horizon task takes a roll target, so the head tilts while its gaze stays on you. Mirrored, as on a call.
